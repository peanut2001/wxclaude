[Anthropic](https://cryptobriefing.com/markets/anthropic/) is working on a new beta feature called Brush for the Claude prompt composer, the box where users type and shape their requests to the AI model.

The feature appeared in development interfaces on November 12, 2026. Anthropic hasn’t announced it, which leaves everyone else guessing what the paintbrush is for.

Brush is labeled as a beta and is described as under active development. It is **expected** to improve how people build prompts in Claude. Beyond that, the company has kept the details to itself.

## What we know about Brush so far

The confirmed facts fit on an index card. Anthropic is developing Brush. It is aimed at the prompt composer. It carries a beta tag.

Everything else is still unconfirmed. As of November 12, 2026, Anthropic had published no announcement about Brush on its blog or in its product updates.

The company has also released no public documentation explaining how the feature works.

The leading theory is that Brush may be connected to an earlier feature called “Sketch.” Under that theory, Brush could be an evolution of Sketch, or possibly its replacement. Neither version has been verified.

The name itself has fueled a second line of conjecture. Some in the observer community think Brush may involve visual or iterative prompt refinement. That would mean adjusting a prompt in stages rather than rewriting it from scratch. That remains an educated guess based on a single word.

## Claude’s interface has been busy lately

Brush is not arriving in isolation. Claude’s interface has picked up other new features recently.

Interactive connectors were introduced around November 10, 2026. That puts them roughly two days ahead of when Brush appeared in development builds.

## What this means for Claude users

For everyday users, the immediate impact is zero. Brush is a beta in development, and there is no public release date or rollout plan on record.

The possible link to Sketch matters too. If Brush replaces an earlier feature, users who relied on Sketch would want to know what changes and what disappears. If it extends Sketch, the transition could be smoother. Anthropic has not addressed either scenario.

Features spotted in development interfaces do not always ship in the form they first appear, and some never ship at all. A beta label is a statement of intent, not a promise.

For developers and teams who use Claude heavily, two interface changes surfacing within days of each other — connectors on November 10 and Brush on November 12 — suggest a steady cadence of product work on the Claude front end.

The next concrete milestone would be an official announcement or documentation from Anthropic. Either would answer the basic questions: what Brush does, whether it relates to Sketch, and who gets access first.

## [Deloitte launches Edisyl after acquiring Flipside Crypto’s AI business](https://cryptobriefing.com/deloitte-launches-edisyl-flipside-crypto-ai/)

The Big Four firm turned a crypto analytics company's AI team into a Boston-based enterprise AI startup built around 'Knowledge Packs'

Deloitte has launched Edisyl Inc., a new enterprise AI company built from the AI business it acquired from Flipside Crypto.

The announcement came on November 12, 2026. It caps a year in which Flipside split itself in two, sold half to one buyer and half to another, and saw its AI side end up inside one of the world’s biggest professional services firms.

## What Edisyl actually does

Edisyl is based in Boston. It pitches itself as a knowledge layer for AI tools, a kind of missing middle piece between a general-purpose model and the specialized work a company needs done.

Edisyl’s answer is a product it calls the Knowledge Pack. Each pack combines domain knowledge annotated by experts with a company’s own proprietary data.

The packs are designed to slot into the AI frameworks companies already use.

The problem Edisyl targets is what the research describes as the “context gap.” That gap is the shortfall that drags down AI accuracy when the work requires expert judgment rather than generic answers.

## How a crypto data firm became an AI startup

Flipside Crypto was founded in 2017. For most of its life, it was a blockchain analytics company, and a sizable one.

The firm built data infrastructure that processed information across more than 40 blockchains. Along the way, it resolved 700 million wallet identities and accumulated around seven trillion rows of data.

Then came the split. On May 19, 2026, Flipside sold its blockchain data business to SonarX. That sale cleared the way for the company to focus entirely on AI.

Two months later, in July 2026, Deloitte completed its acquisition of Flipside’s AI business. The deal brought over the entire 24-person operational team.

That team includes Dave Balter, Flipside’s former CEO, who now serves as a co-founder of Edisyl.

So the timeline runs like this: the data business went to SonarX in May, the AI business went to Deloitte in July, and Edisyl launched as an independent entity in November.

## Deloitte’s venture playbook

Edisyl sits within a broader effort at Deloitte. Its D71 Ventures arm aims to nurture AI-native companies by providing both financial and operational support.

The acquisition also follows a familiar logic in tech deals: buy the team, not just the product. Bringing over all 24 operational staff means Edisyl starts with people who have already spent years wrangling massive, messy datasets.

## What this means

For the crypto industry, the Flipside story is a case study in how blockchain analytics talent can migrate into broader enterprise AI. Flipside’s blockchain data operation didn’t disappear, either. It simply changed hands to SonarX, which now owns that infrastructure.

For Deloitte, Edisyl is a bet on a specific theory of enterprise AI. The theory holds that raw model power is not the bottleneck. Context is.
